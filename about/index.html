About Open Seforim

Open readers for Hebrew and Judaica texts.

What this is

Open Seforim publishes reading editions of Hebrew and Judaica texts, and of older studies about them. Each work gives the source text, a link from each page to the scan it was read from, and an English translation next to it. Most of the works have never been translated into English, and some have no digital text at all.

How the editions are made

  • The source comes first. The text is taken from a named printed edition. Each page links to the library scan, so you can check any reading against the print.
  • Machine reading is checked. Where the text was read by OCR, the readings are checked against the scan, and the corrections are kept with the data.
  • Translations say what they are. Many translations are drafts made with AI models and directed by the editor. Each work states its method and status (for example "Draft, not reviewed"), and marks uncertain renderings and references that are not yet verified.
  • Everything is open. The texts, the data and the scripts that build each site are on GitHub.

Use and licence

The transcriptions, translations, notes and data are under CC BY-SA 4.0: you may copy, adapt and republish them, also commercially, if you name the source and share your version under the same licence. The code is under the MIT License. The printed source texts are in the public domain; the scans belong to the libraries that hold them, under the rights statement shown on each work. The footer of each work gives a citation you can copy.

Corrections

Found a misreading or a wrong translation? Every work has a "Report a mistake" link that opens an issue on its GitHub repository. Corrections are welcome.

Who

Open Seforim is edited by Ezra Brand, who writes about Talmud, Jewish texts and digital humanities at ezrabrand.com.
