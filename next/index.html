The English History of the Jews (JPS, 1891–98) translates the narrative but leaves out the Noten, the long source-critical excursuses at the end of each volume. They are where Graetz argues his dates and readings, and they are still cited. They have never been in English.

Details

Scans: Every volume is on archive.org, for example the 1900 edition from Illinois (vol. 10) and the Toronto set (vol. 10).

Translate the notes only, linked to the JPS narrative. Pilot: the notes to vol. 10 (Sabbatai Zevi to the Emden–Eybeschütz controversy), which fit the Emden reader. Use one edition throughout (the last revised by Graetz himself).

Ḥayyim Vital, Sefer ha-Ḥezyonot

ספר החזיונות
written c. 1610s, untitled (the name is from his grandson Moshe Vital, used by Ḥida); abridged and censored as Shivḥei Rabbi Ḥayyim Vital, Ostrog 1826/27 (printed at Slavita; reprinted about 12 times up to 1953); complete ed. A. Z. Aescoly, Jerusalem: Mossad HaRav Kook 1954, from a photograph of the autograph; critical ed. M. M. Faierstein, Jerusalem: Yad Ben-Zvi 2005/6 · Hebrew

The only first-person diary of a major kabbalist of Safed: his life, his years with the Ari, his dreams and the dreams others had about him, and the Kuntres Shoresh Nishmati on his own soul's lineage. A key source for Safed in the 16th century and for the self-image of a mystic, and a first-person text like Emden's Megillat Sefer.

Details

Scans: Ostrog 1826/27 (Shivḥei R. Ḥayyim Vital, abridged and censored): HebrewBooks 30687 (linked from the Hebrew Wikipedia article; not opened). A later printing, 1863, 32 leaves (Penn catalogue record). Aescoly 1954: on Otzar HaChochma (subscription). The autograph was held in Livorno (Toaff, from the estate of Elia Benamozegh); a photograph of it is in the Mossad HaRav Kook library (found by Y. Avivi around 2000). Check the NLI (Ktiv) for a film of the manuscript.

Existing work: Not on Sefaria or Wikisource. English: M. M. Faierstein, Jewish Mystical Autobiographies: Book of Visions and Book of Secrets (Paulist Press, Classics of Western Spirituality, 1999), from Aescoly's text; in copyright. Hebrew critical edition: Faierstein, Sefer ha-Ḥezyonot: Yomano shel R. Ḥayyim Vital (Yad Ben-Zvi, 2005/6), with an English summary; in copyright. N. M. Manzur printed a private edition based on Aescoly, with its own cuts.

Five parts in the printed editions: (1) a diary of events, (2–3) dreams, (4) Kuntres Shoresh Nishmati, (5) a mixed part, only partly by Vital, which Faierstein leaves out. Y. Avivi argues that the book is two separate works: the diary with its dreams (parts 1–3) and the Kuntres. Copyright: Vital's text is old, but the full text exists only in modern editions. Aescoly died in 1948 (check), so his 1954 edition is likely public domain in the EU, but probably not in the US (an Israeli book of 1954). The 1826/27 printing is public domain but cut and censored. Options: a reader of the 1826/27 text with the missing passages marked; or a new transcription from the autograph photograph, if a scan of it can be found. Do not copy Aescoly's or Faierstein's notes, and do not use Faierstein's translation. Suggested by the owner, 26-Nov-2026.
