About Open Seforim

Hebrew and Judaica texts, and the older scholarship about them, as open reading editions with English translations. Most of these works have never been translated, and many have no digital text at all.

How the editions are made

  1. Scan. Each work starts from a library scan of a named printed edition in the public domain. Every page of the reading edition links back to its scan.
  2. Digitize. AI models read the page images into text: vision-language models such as Claude for Hebrew, Latin and German, and OCR models such as Kraken for Syriac. A second reading finds the doubtful words, which are checked against the scan.
  3. Translate. AI models translate the text section by section, with the source alongside and with lists of names, terms and abbreviations. Uncertain renderings are marked ⚠.
  4. Publish. Source text, scan and translation appear side by side, with search and indexes. The texts, data and the code that builds each site are open on GitHub.

The editor chooses the works and the sources, directs and checks the process, and corrects the results. A whole book can go from scan to a searchable, translated edition in days or weeks rather than years, and it stays open to correction afterwards. Each work's footer states its source edition and method.

Use and corrections

Texts and translations are under CC BY-SA 4.0 and code under MIT: copy, adapt and republish freely, with credit. Each work's footer has a citation to copy and a "Report a mistake" link, which opens an issue on GitHub.
