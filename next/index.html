Wilhelm Bacher, Die Agada der palästinensischen Amoräer

3 vols., Strassburg 1892–99 · German, with Hebrew and Aramaic

The aggadah of the amoraim of the Land of Israel, sage by sage, from the generation after Judah ha-Nasi to the end of the amoraic period. The fullest guide to the Yerushalmi and the classical midrashim.

Details

Scans: archive.org dieagadaderpalas01bach, archive.org dieagadaderpalas02bach, archive.org dieagadaderpalas03bach (University of Illinois, 300 ppi); also archive.org dieagadaderpalae0001bach to 0003 (a reprint).

Existing work: Hebrew: translated by A. Z. Rabinowitz (Jaffa, Berlin and Tel Aviv: Devir, 1924–); Rabinowitz died in 1945, so the translation is public domain in the EU; check US status for volumes after 1930. Aggadat Amora'e Eretz Yisra'el scans: archive.org agadatamoraeeret111bach and the next parts. No English translation found.

About 2,000 pages: a long project. Pairs with Frankel's Mevo ha-Yerushalmi.

Wilhelm Bacher, Die Agada der Tannaiten

2 vols., Strassburg 1884–90; vol. 1 2nd ed. 1903 · German, with Hebrew and Aramaic

The aggadic teachings of every tanna, sage by sage, from Hillel and Shammai to Judah ha-Nasi, with the sources in full. Still the standard collection for rabbinic thought and the sages' biographies, and never in English.

Details

Scans: 2nd ed. vol. 1 (1903) and vol. 2: archive.org dieagadadertanna01bach, archive.org dieagadadertanna02bach (Toronto, 400–500 ppi). Others: archive.org dieagadadertann00bachgoog (1890), archive.org dieagadadertanna0000bach. Index of Bible verses (1902): archive.org dieagadadertanna00bach.

Existing work: Hebrew: translated by A. Z. Rabinowitz (Jaffa, Berlin and Tel Aviv: Devir, 1920s); Rabinowitz died in 1945, so the translation is public domain in the EU; check US status for volumes after 1930. Project Ben-Yehuda has Aggadot ha-Tannaim vol. 1 part 1 (work 64894) and the translator's introduction; the other parts are scans on archive.org (for example archive.org agadothatanaim11bach). No English translation found.

German with many Hebrew quotations on the same lines: plan a vision model or a two-pass Kraken run. The Hebrew translation can help to check the Hebrew quotations.

Zacharias Frankel, Darkhe ha-Mishnah

דרכי המשנה
Leipzig 1859 (Latin title Hodegetica in Mischnam); Tosafot u-mafteaḥ, Leipzig 1867; reprinted Warsaw 1923 and later · Hebrew

The first critical history of the Mishnah and its sages, by the head of the Breslau seminary. Its historical treatment of the Oral Law caused one of the great controversies of 19th-century Judaism (Hirsch, Auerbach and others answered it). Still read, and never translated into English.

Details

Scans: 1859: HathiTrust hvd.hwmg1g and hvd.hwmg1h (Harvard, full view). Supplement 1867: HathiTrust uc1.b3938825. 1923 reprint with the supplement: HathiTrust osu.32435052665197 (full view in the US only). Nothing on archive.org under its title.

Existing work: Not on Sefaria, Wikisource or Project Ben-Yehuda. No English translation found; studies only (for example D. Mackler, Jewish Studies Quarterly 28, 2021).

A Hebrew-only text: Kraken with a Hebrew model should do the OCR at no cost per page. Base text 1859 plus the 1867 supplement.

Heinrich Graetz, Geschichte der Juden: the Noten

11 vols., Leipzig 1853–76, later editions to 1909 · German, with Hebrew

The English History of the Jews (JPS, 1891–98) translates the narrative but leaves out the Noten, the long source-critical excursuses at the end of each volume. They are where Graetz argues his dates and readings, and they are still cited. They have never been in English.

Details

Scans: Every volume is on archive.org, for example the 1900 edition from Illinois (vol. 10) and the Toronto set (vol. 10).

Existing work: English: the JPS abridgement (6 vols.) without the notes, public domain. Hebrew: Divre yeme Yisra'el, trans. S. P. Rabinowitz (Warsaw, 1890s), with notes; check its status. Not on Wikisource.

Translate the notes only, linked to the JPS narrative. Pilot: the notes to vol. 10 (Sabbatai Zevi to the Emden–Eybeschütz controversy), which fit the Emden reader. Use one edition throughout (the last revised by Graetz himself).

Ḥida, Ma'agal Tov

מעגל טוב
partial printings 1879 and Przemyśl 1885; complete ed. A. Freimann, Mekize Nirdamim, pt. 1 Berlin 1921, pt. 2 Jerusalem 1934 (220 pp.; photo reprint 1967) · Hebrew

His travel diary as an emissary of Hebron in the 1750s and the 1770s: Egypt, Italy, Germany, Holland, France, England and Tunisia, with Versailles, the royal library in Paris, a cabinet of curiosities in Amsterdam, and the death of his wife, which he mourned in secret. A first-person 18th-century Hebrew text like Megillat Sefer, with some of the same communities.

Details

Scans: Freimann's complete edition (1921–34): HebrewBooks 21838 (linked from the Hebrew Wikipedia article; not opened, as HebrewBooks blocks automated browsers); on HathiTrust it is search-only (uc1.$b745676, coo.31924070058247), and so is the 1967 reprint. Partial printing, Przemyśl 1885, full view: archive.org maagaltob00azul (University of Illinois, 650 ppi, OCR text layer; the same scan is HathiTrust uiuo.ark:/13960/t19m0jx18) and HathiTrust uc1.g0001630797 (University of California). National Library of Israel: authority record.

Existing work: Not on Sefaria, Wikisource or Project Ben-Yehuda, so the text needs OCR (Kraken with a Hebrew model, as for Emden). English: B. Cymerman, The Diaries of Rabbi Ha'im Yosef David Azulai (Jerusalem, 1997), in copyright; check how complete it is. French: Les voyageurs juifs du XVIIIe siècle (1997). Three Hebrew editions appeared in 2022, all in copyright: A. Raviv (Talmim; vol. 1, first journey, with photographs), Mekhon ha-Maor (complete, with more readings from the author's manuscript), and D. A. Mandelbaum (corrections from the manuscript, Freimann's page numbers in the margin) (forum thread on the editions). Study: O. Cohen, Sevav holekh ha-ruaḥ (Magnes, 2023).

Base text: Freimann's edition. Freimann died in 1948, so the edition is public domain in the EU (from 2019); part 1 (1921) is public domain in the US, while HathiTrust treats the 1934 volume as in copyright in the US. The diary text itself is 18th-century. Do not copy readings, notes or added passages from the 2022 editions. The 1885 printing has only part of the text but a clean full-view scan with an OCR layer, good for an OCR test. His son Isaiah held the diary back from print because it spoke badly of living people; the diary records his bouts of melancholy. Suggested in the review of the Emden reader.

Leopold Zunz, Die gottesdienstlichen Vorträge der Juden

Berlin 1832; 2nd ed. by N. Brüll, Frankfurt am Main 1892 · German, with Hebrew and Aramaic quotations

The founding work of the Wissenschaft des Judentums: the first historical account of the midrash and the synagogue sermon, from the Bible to Zunz's own day. Still the starting point for the dating and history of the midrashic collections, and never translated into English.

Details

Scans: 1832: archive.org bub_gb_ONEXAAAAIAAJ, archive.org diegottesdienst02zunzgoog. 1892 (2nd ed., about 540 pp.): archive.org bub_gb_INgLAAAAYAAJ (Harvard), archive.org diegottesdienst01zunzgoog, archive.org bub_gb_PWY-AAAAYAAJ_2.

Existing work: No English translation found. Hebrew: Ha-Derashot be-Yisra'el, ed. Ḥ. Albeck (Jerusalem, 1947; 2nd ed. 1954, reprinted 1974), with Albeck's additions; in copyright. Not on Sefaria or Wikisource.

Base text: the 1892 edition, which adds Brüll's notes from Zunz's own copy (both men died long ago). German with many Hebrew quotations on the same lines, so plan for a vision model or a two-pass OCR. Do not use Albeck's Hebrew additions.

Berlin 1865; Nachtrag, Berlin 1867 · German, with Hebrew

The catalogue of the payyetanim: hundreds of liturgical poets, with their poems, dates and places. Still the standard reference behind Davidson's Thesaurus and every modern edition of piyyut, and never translated.

Details

Scans: 1865, about 690 pp.: archive.org literaturgeschic00zunzuoft (Toronto), archive.org literaturgeschi01zunzgoog, archive.org literaturgeschi00zunzgoog, archive.org 11007532bsb (Munich).

Existing work: No translation found. Not on Wikisource or Sefaria.

A reference work, not a narrative: a reader with a searchable index of poets and first lines may be worth more than a running translation. Pairs with the companion volumes Die synagogale Poesie des Mittelalters (1855) and Die Ritus des synagogalen Gottesdienstes (1859).
