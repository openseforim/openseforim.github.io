Leone Modena, Ari Nohem

ארי נוהם
written 1639; ed. J. Fürst, Leipzig 1840; ed. N. S. Libowitz, Jerusalem 1929 (reprinted 1970) · Hebrew

The first sustained attack on Kabbalah and on the antiquity of the Zohar, by a rabbi: thirty-one chapters arguing that Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai did not write the Zohar. A key text in the history of Jewish criticism, and the background to Emden's later Mitpaḥat Sefarim.

Details

Scans: Fürst, Leipzig 1840, 97 pp. with a German title page ("Streitschrift über die Echtheit des Sohar"): HathiTrust uc1.a0012351383, full view everywhere. Libowitz, Jerusalem 1929, with notes by S. Bloch ha-Levi and M. Steinschneider: HathiTrust mdp.39015069691460, uc1.$b378523, umn.31951003004925h, full view in the US only. The 1970 reprint is search-only. Manuscript: Bodleian, MS Reggio 34. Nothing on archive.org.

Existing work: No transcribed text found: Project Ben-Yehuda does not have it, so the text needs OCR (Kraken with a Hebrew model, as for Emden). English: Y. Dweck, The Scandal of Kabbalah: Leon Modena, Jewish Mysticism, Early Modern Venice (Princeton, 2011), a study with long quotations (check whether it gives a full translation); in copyright.

Base text: Fürst 1840 (public domain everywhere). Use Libowitz 1929 only to check readings, since its notes may still be in copyright outside the US.

Checked: 23-Nov-2026: HathiTrust, archive.org, Open Library and the Project Ben-Yehuda catalogue; HebrewBooks and the NLI not checked (HebrewBooks refused the automated search) · added 23-Nov-2026 · Source file

Leone Modena, Ḥayyei Yehudah

חיי יהודה
ed. A. Kahana, Kiev 1911 (title page 1912) · Hebrew

The first full autobiography in Hebrew: a Venetian rabbi, preacher and gambler tells of his family, his debts, his sons' deaths, plague, and the ghetto. A first-person text like Emden's Megillat Sefer.

Details

Scans: Kahana, Kiev 1911–12, 71 pp.: archive.org sepherhayyeyehud01mode (Duke University Libraries, 360 ppi); archive.org 3855186.0001.001.umich.edu (University of Michigan). The autograph manuscript is in the Bodleian Library, Oxford.

Existing work: Project Ben-Yehuda has the whole text from Kahana's edition (work 31022, about 230 KB), so no OCR is needed; check it against the scans. English: M. R. Cohen, The Autobiography of a Seventeenth-Century Venetian Rabbi (Princeton, 1988), with notes by H. E. Adelman and B. C. I. Ravid, in copyright. The critical edition by D. Carpi (Tel Aviv, 1985) is in copyright and must not be used.

Kahana's edition is from the Bodleian autograph. A good companion to the Emden reader, and short enough to finish.

Checked: 23-Nov-2026: Project Ben-Yehuda text and archive.org scans · added 23-Nov-2026 · Source file

Judah al-Ḥarizi, Taḥkemoni

תחכמוני
Constantinople 1578; ed. P. de Lagarde, Göttingen 1883; ed. A. Kaminka, Warsaw 1899 · Hebrew

The classic Hebrew maqamat: 50 chapters of rhymed prose and poems, and the model for Immanuel of Rome's Maḥberot, so the two would make a pair.

Details

Scans: Lagarde 1883 (archive.org tahkemoni00hari, 400 ppi, IIIF; also HathiTrust uc1.b4612172); Kaminka 1899 (archive.org, 333 ppi; HathiTrust hvd.hwmiag, mdp.39015093249079); Constantinople 1578 (HathiTrust uc1.b4616630); Amsterdam 1729 (HathiTrust mdp.39015014644572); German translation of the first maqamat by S. I. Kaempf, Berlin 1845 (archive.org). Lagarde or Kaminka would be the base text.

Existing work: English translations by V. E. Reichert (1965–73) and D. S. Segal (2001), both in copyright: the value is an open Hebrew text and an open translation. The critical edition by Yahalom and Katsumata (2010) is in copyright and must not be used. Project Ben-Yehuda has the vocalized Hebrew of the introduction and 49 of the 50 gates (checked 23-Nov-2026); gate 35 is not there and would need OCR from a scan.

Pilot started 23-Nov-2026: gate 40 (the pen and the sword), Hebrew from Project Ben-Yehuda, line-aligned English.

Samuel Bochart, Hierozoicon

London 1663; Leipzig 1793–96, ed. E. F. C. Rosenmüller · Latin, with Greek, Hebrew, Aramaic, Syriac and Arabic

The standard early-modern study of every animal in the Bible, and a main source for Lewysohn and Löw. Suggested by a reader as "very useful (but immensely more complex)".

Details

Scans: Munich (MDZ/BSB), Rosenmüller edition with IIIF and an hOCR layer: bsb10353453, bsb10353454, bsb10353455. Also Leiden 1692 (bsb11203288–89), Leiden 1712 (bsb10409819–20), Frankfurt 1675 (bsb11935761–62).

Existing work: No modern translation found, full or partial, in any language. Study: Zur Shalev, Sacred Words and Worlds (Brill 2012), ch. 4.

About 1 million words; much Arabic in Arabic type and a second layer of notes by Rosenmüller. Now a reader, in progress.
